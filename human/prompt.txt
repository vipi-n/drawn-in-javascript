PROMPT — "STILL CURIOUS: the history of human evolution in under a minute"
(animated short, 56 s, 1080x1080, 24 fps, browser player, seamless loop with synthesized music)

This file holds (1) the full production prompt that describes the finished film, so it can be recreated, and
(2) the requests that were used to arrive at it (also quoted in the repo README).

====================================================================================================
PART 1 — THE PROMPT
====================================================================================================

The history of human evolution in under one minute. Draw every frame in JavaScript on an HTML canvas in the same
hand-drawn crayon style as the dinosaur film, and add music. One character evolves from an ape to a modern human as
he walks through 7 million years.

FORMAT
- 1080 x 1080, 24 fps, 56 s; the end wraps into the start so the film loops forever.
- Each scene is a pure function of time, scene(t), so any frame can be drawn instantly and the slider is exact.

LOOK
- Line boil every 2 frames, rough crayon hatching with paper grain on warm cream paper.
- One rubber-hose rig with simple inverse kinematics and an "evo" value from 0 (ape) to 1 (modern human): the
  character gradually stands taller, loses fur and changes posture. He changes clothes each time he passes behind a
  bush, an obelisk or a phone box.
- The camera zooms into objects to move between scenes: a spark becomes a campfire, the cave mouth goes dark, a
  painted sun becomes the real sun.

STORY
1. 7 million years ago: an ape swings through the jungle, falls off a branch, sees stars, and stands up on two legs.
2. 2.6 million years ago: he knocks two stones together and makes the first hand axe. A spark flies off...
3. 1 million years ago: ...and becomes a campfire. A sabre-tooth cat creeps out of the dark; a torch drives it away.
4. 300,000 to 70,000 years ago: the ice age — mammoths, a blizzard, people huddled around a tiny fire.
5. 40,000 years ago: cave art — a hand stencil, a painted mammoth, a painted sun.
6. 12,000 years ago to 57 years ago, one continuous walk: farming (a dog joins in), pushing a cart past the
   pyramids, waving at a sailing ship, a city street, then a rocket.
7. The Moon: one small step.
8. Today: a toddler's first wobbly steps under the same tree the ape hung from. A butterfly lands on the child's
   hand: "still curious". The film loops back to the start.

MUSIC
- Built sample by sample in js/music.js: marimba, glockenspiel, plucked strings, piano, pads, bass and drums with a
  small reverb, plus sound effects (roar, fire crackle, stone clacks, dog bark, rocket rumble and more) timed to the
  action; the track wraps into its start so the loop is seamless.

FILES AND CONTROLS
- index.html; js/ink.js (canvas, wobbly ink, crayon hatching, text), js/kit.js (shared props), js/people.js (the
  ape-to-human rig, kid, sabre-tooth, dog, butterfly), js/world.js (trees, fire, snow, cave art, pyramids, ship,
  city, rocket, moon), js/scenes.js (timeline and captions), js/music.js, js/main.js (playback, controls, export).
- index.html?t=36 opens the film paused at 36 s; index.html?sheet=0,15,0.5&cols=6 shows a contact sheet.

====================================================================================================
PART 2 — HOW IT WAS PROMPTED (the requests, in order)
====================================================================================================

1. "The history of human evolution in under one minute. Draw every frame of this animation in JavaScript. Here is
   the video reference, I pasted the video in the current app folder. I want the visuals and animation like this.
   Also add music to this animation."

2. "Make the same kind of video for human evolution. I gave the reference so you can see all things like design,
   animation etc. Make it for humans, and don't change the dinosaur one, keep it as well."
   -> The film above, made with Claude Opus 5.5 in Cursor. The reference video was a hand-drawn, crayon-style
      animation used only for visual style; it is not included.
