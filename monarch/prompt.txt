PROMPT — "MONARCH: the life cycle of a butterfly"
(animated short, 2 min 17 s, 1:1 square 1080x1080, 30 fps, stereo 48 kHz, original music + sound design)

This file holds (1) the full production prompt that describes the finished video, so it can be recreated, and
(2) the request that was used to arrive at it.


====================================================================================================================
PART 1 — THE PROMPT
====================================================================================================================

Create a richly coloured, cinematic motion-animation film about the complete life cycle of the monarch butterfly:
the egg laid on milkweed, the caterpillar and its five growth stages, the chrysalis and the transformation inside it,
the emergence of the butterfly, its summer life, the four generations of a year, the long migration of the
"super generation" to the mountains of central Mexico, the winter there, and the journey home that starts the cycle
again. Every step is SHOWN with animation; text is limited to short chapter tags, short fact chips and counters.
The music changes with every situation and carries one recurring theme through the whole story.

FORMAT
- 1080 x 1080 square, 30 fps, 137.5 seconds (55 bars at 96 BPM, one bar = 2.5 s), stereo 48 kHz.
- Every frame is drawn with JavaScript code (Node.js + canvas). No photos, no video footage, no image assets.
- Watermark: X logo + "@vipinxtwt" in the bottom-left corner of every frame.

LOOK
- Luminous storybook illustration: clean vector shapes with soft gradient shading, thin dark outlines on the
  animals, glowing light (sun discs, god rays, bloom), floating pollen motes, out-of-focus bokeh discs and blurred
  foreground plants for depth of field, a warm paper grain and a soft vignette over every frame.
- Each chapter has its own colour script: violet-to-gold dawn; fresh green macro leaf worlds; golden hour;
  moonlit blue night; an indigo x-ray world with cyan and magenta light; a pink-gold dawn for the emergence;
  bright summer blue; autumn oranges; misty blue-green mountains; a twilight-violet finale with gold.
- TREES are painted, not geometric: every broadleaf tree is a recursively branched trunk whose crown is built from
  scalloped leafy clumps shaded as ONE lit volume (light from the upper left, dark underside, thousands of leaf
  dabs), with a full rounded skirt of foliage hiding the fork (no lollipop or umbrella crowns); summer, spring and
  four autumn palettes (red, orange, yellow, mixed) in oak, tall and bush shapes. Oyamel firs are 38 tiers of
  needle clumps around a dark core. Every sprite has a pre-blurred twin for depth of field and is tinted by the
  time of day.
- LANDSCAPES use atmospheric perspective: each layer is painted offscreen and hazed toward the horizon colour.
  The meadow has a far range with a continuous woodland edge, mid hills with patchwork fields, groves, lone trees
  and natural hedgerows (varying height, gaps), and a near flowery slope with big framing trees that sway.
  Clouds are soft shaded sprites, tinted gold-over-rose at dawn and dusk and slate at night. A lens flare
  (ghosts strung through the frame centre) follows the sun in every sunny shot.

CHARACTERS (all rigged in code)
- MONARCH BUTTERFLY: anatomically patterned wings — orange cells divided by black veins, black margins with two
  rows of white dots, a black forewing tip with orange sub-apical spots, dark scales near the body; the underside is
  paler buff-orange with white-edged veins. Black body with white dots, compound eyes, clubbed antennae, legs and a
  coiled proboscis. Each wing is drawn flat and projected through a 3D pose (heading, roll, pitch, flap angle), so
  flapping, banking and turning foreshorten the real pattern and the correct side (top or underside) shows. Males
  have thinner veins and a black scent spot on each hind wing. Extra states: crumpled wings that expand, a swollen
  abdomen that shrinks, an abdomen that curls to lay eggs, the proboscis uncoiling into a flower, worn wing edges
  on an old traveller, backlit "stained-glass" glow.
- CATERPILLAR: 13 rings plus head laid along any path (leaf edge, eggshell, twig underside, J-curve); bold yellow,
  black and white bands, black-and-yellow striped head with chewing mandibles, two pairs of black filaments that
  sway (long at the front, short at the back), true legs and prolegs; a peristaltic wave runs from tail to head
  when it crawls. First instar pale grey-green with a large black head; stripes and filaments grow with each instar.
- EGG: cream, upright, ribbed, about 1.2 mm tall; the black head darkens the tip before hatching.
- CHRYSALIS: jade green lantern shape on a black cremaster hooked into a white silk pad, a black line set with
  gold dots around its top third, gold spots lower down, glossy highlights; it can be elongated (fresh pupa),
  wriggle, turn clear to show the folded orange wing, crack, and remain as an empty split shell.
- BLUE JAY: crest, black necklace, flapping wings, opening beak, gagging head-shake.
- PLANTS: common milkweed (broad leaves with veins that can be eaten away bite by bite, pink flower umbels, pods
  that split and release silky seeds), coneflowers, goldenrod, blazing star / bluebonnets, daisies, grass.
- PLACES: a parallax dawn meadow; macro leaf surfaces with twinkling dew; a twig at golden hour and under the
  moon; the migration seen from high above as a ground plane in true perspective (staggered patchwork plots with
  furrows, red-barn farmsteads with silos, a country road, a winding river glinting in the sun, hundreds of
  fall-coloured trees in woods and hedgerows, drifting cloud shadows, haze to the horizon); misty Mexican
  mountains whose ridges are clothed in fir sprites, with three ranks of big oyamel firs in front that fill with
  hanging bunches of roosting monarchs (and orange basking wings once the sun hits); a close-up of three sagging
  fir branches at three depths draped with hundreds of real rig butterflies, a few live ones shivering their
  wings to warm up and then opening them; a stylised map of North America (coasts, Great Lakes, mountain ranges,
  CANADA / UNITED STATES / MEXICO).
- LIFE EVERYWHERE: a fuzzy bumblebee works the flowers (egg-laying and nectar scenes); small birds cross the
  summer sky and a V of geese flies south over the autumn meadow; leaf crumbs tumble from the caterpillar's
  mandibles while it eats; every butterfly's antennae sway and twitch; flying butterflies bob with each
  downstroke.

MOTION DESIGN
- The camera never stands still: keyframed moves with hand-held drift, parallax layers and impact shakes.
- A macro-to-wide pull-back from a backlit wing; zoom-throughs between scales; whip pans; iris wipes into and out
  of the x-ray world; springy pop-ins with overshoot; burst rings and flashes on key moments; sparkle trails behind
  flying butterflies; time-lapse skies with racing clocks.
- Events land on the beat of the music.
- Five motion-design wipes, each revealing the next shot behind a moving tilted seam: a giant out-of-focus monarch
  sweeping past the lens (title -> egg, first flight -> summer), a living curtain of monarchs with huge soft ones
  near the lens (-> four generations, -> the migration), and a bank of cloud (-> the mountains of Mexico).

STORY / SHOT LIST
0–10 s    TITLE. Out of black, a backlit wing glows like stained glass; the camera pulls back to a monarch basking
          with breathing wings on a pink milkweed flower at dawn (rays, mist, bokeh). She flutters, lifts off and
          loops around the title "MONARCH" / "the life cycle of a butterfly", trailing sparkles, then dives into
          the meadow.
10–20 s   1 THE EGG. She lands on a milkweed leaf, tastes it with her feet (ripple rings), curls her abdomen over
          the edge and lays one egg (glint). She leaves; the camera swings under the leaf to the egg: a macro of the
          ribbed egg with a "1 mm" scale bar, a DAY 1 -> 4 badge, an x-ray lens on the curled larva forming inside,
          the tip darkening, a tremble, chewing.
20–25 s   2 THE CATERPILLAR. The pale first instar chews out of the top, climbs down the shell, turns round and eats
          its eggshell; top view: it walks a ring, cutting the leaf's latex veins (white latex beads), then eats the
          disc inside.
25–37.5 s FIVE STAGES in time-lapse on a milkweed leaf: munching the edge away, frass dropping on the beat; at
          every downbeat it freezes, molts (old skin and head capsule left behind) and springs out bigger and more
          boldly striped; STAGE 1 -> 5 badge, a "1 cm" ruler that shrinks as the camera pulls out, "×2,000 heavier
          in two weeks".
37.5–42.5 THE JAY. A shadow sweeps over; a blue jay grabs the caterpillar; an x-ray iris shows the milkweed poison
          glowing purple inside it; the jay gags, spits it out and flies off shaking its head; warning-colour rings
          pulse round the unharmed caterpillar, which goes back to eating.
42.5–50 s 3 THE CHRYSALIS. Golden hour: the fat caterpillar wanders along the underside of a twig, spins a silk
          pad (glinting thread), grips it, drops and curls into a J; night falls with stars, the moon and a clock
          racing through the hours.
50–57.5 s Under the moon the skin splits behind the head, is wriggled up in steps and spun off; the soft green pupa
          shortens into a jade chrysalis while gold dots sparkle into being.
57.5–67.5 4 METAMORPHOSIS. Iris into an indigo x-ray world: the old body melts into a swirl of light; imaginal
          discs glow and bloom into wing, eye, antennae, legs and proboscis while a DAY 1 -> 10 badge counts; the
          wing outline draws itself with its veins, then floods orange. Back in colour the chrysalis has turned
          clear, showing the folded wing.
67.5–77.5 5 THE BUTTERFLY. Dawn: the case cracks; she slides out, grips the empty shell and hangs with a fat
          abdomen and tiny crumpled wings; fluid pulses into the veins and the wings expand while the abdomen
          shrinks; a lens shows the two halves of the proboscis zipping together; a clock counts 2 hours of drying
          while she opens and closes her wings.
77.5–82.5 FIRST FLIGHT across a wide morning sky, other monarchs in the distance.
82.5–92.5 SUMMER. She sips nectar with the uncoiled proboscis; female and male side by side (♀ ♂, rings on the
          male's two scent spots); a courtship spiral; a mated pair; four beat-cuts of eggs laid one per leaf with a
          counter racing to 500.
92.5–100  6 FOUR GENERATIONS on the map with a season wheel (March -> September): generations 1, 2 and 3 move
          north from Texas to the Great Lakes and Canada (spinning life-cycle icons); the golden 4th generation is
          born in late summer; bars compare "weeks" with "months".
100–112.5 7 THE GREAT MIGRATION. Autumn meadow: pods split and seeds drift away; the long-lived monarch rises and
          others join; high above fall colours a river of monarchs streams south, some circling up a thermal; a sun
          compass shows the heading; on the map the routes converge through Texas on the mountains of central
          Mexico (pin), a western route reaches coastal California, and a counter runs 0 -> 4,800 km.
112.5–122.5 8 WINTER IN MEXICO. Misty blue mountains of oyamel firs fill with streams of monarchs; close-up of
          branches hung with clustered butterflies, light snow, a counter rolling up to "millions"; sunlight breaks
          through, wings open, and at the downbeat the whole forest bursts into the air in an orange blizzard.
122.5–127.5 9 THE JOURNEY HOME. Spring map: they fly north into Texas; a worn old female lays one egg on a fresh
          milkweed sprout among bluebonnets, then flies off into the light and fades; the egg glows.
127.5–137.5 FINALE. A gold ring draws itself; four stations pop on (egg, caterpillar, chrysalis, butterfly) with
          chevrons, a running spark and four tiny butterflies orbiting (four generations); "MONARCH" / "four
          generations, one great journey"; the butterfly flies into the lens; end card "plant milkweed / every
          monarch begins on one"; fade to black.

FACTS SHOWN (as short chips and counters)
- Eggs are laid only on milkweed, one per leaf, up to ~500 per female; an egg is smaller than a pinhead (~1.2 mm)
  and hatches in about 3–5 days; the first meal is the eggshell.
- The caterpillar eats only milkweed, grows through 5 stages with 4 molts, and becomes about 2,000× heavier in
  roughly two weeks; milkweed toxins make it poisonous to birds, and its bright bands are a warning.
- It spins a silk pad, hangs in a "J" for about a day, then sheds its skin into a chrysalis for about 10 days, where
  the body is rebuilt from clusters of cells (imaginal discs).
- The new butterfly pumps fluid into its wings, zips its two proboscis halves together and dries for ~2 hours.
- Adults sip nectar; males have two black scent spots; summer generations live 2–6 weeks; the 4th generation lives
  up to 8 months, flies up to 4,800 km to central Mexico steering by the sun, and millions cluster on fir trees for
  the winter; in spring they fly north, lay eggs and die, and their descendants continue the cycle.

MUSIC (original, synthesized, 96 BPM, D major; one "monarch theme")
- Dawn and title: Dmaj9 pad swell, celesta, harp arpeggios, then the theme on strings and glockenspiel with
  choir, brass and timpani as the title lands.
- Egg: a music-box lullaby over pizzicato, celesta sixteenths, a bell and tick for each day, a tremolo for the
  tremble.
- Caterpillar: a bouncy band — marimba ostinato, upright-bass boom-chick, shaker and claps, the theme as a cartoon
  bassoon, xylophone doubling; every molt adds a layer (rubbery stretch, pop, cymbal, brass stab); munching on the
  eighth notes; a counter of ticks for ×2,000; the music sucks away before the bird.
- Jay: D-diminished tremolo, timpani roll, the grab hit, x-ray drone, a comic "wah-wah" and spit, then the band
  bounces back.
- Golden hour and J: nylon-guitar fingerpicking and strings; a glittering shimmer for the silk; a falling harp
  glissando for the curl; a celesta lullaby and accelerating clock at nightfall.
- Chrysalis: mysterious Em9 / Cmaj7 harp and pads, rubbery wriggles, timpani on the twist, a choir as it takes shape,
  bells for each gold dot.
- Metamorphosis: a pulsing synth arpeggio and heartbeat that grow brighter over ten days, fizzing sparkles, five
  bells for the discs, a choir-and-brass swell when the wing floods with colour.
- Emergence: A-pedal tremolo, a crack and timpani, then the theme in slow motion on strings while the wings pump
  up, a zipper for the proboscis, a flute while it dries.
- First flight: the theme on full orchestra — strings in two octaves, brass, choir, timpani, toms.
- Summer: guitar, flute (the theme), shaker; harp spirals for the courtship; bells for the eggs.
- Generations: pizzicato clockwork, a rising bell per generation, choir and sparkles for the golden 4th.
- Migration: autumn guitar and drifting celesta, then a driving B-minor string ostinato with toms, brass and the
  theme in minor; ticking distance counter; arrival hit.
- Mexico: choir, marimba tremolo and nylon guitar over wind in the firs; a hush of bells; a harp glissando and timpani
  roll as the sun breaks through; on the downbeat the whole orchestra plays the theme while a roar of wings rises.
- Journey home: flute and guitar; a bell for the last egg; a choir as she fades into the light.
- Finale: the theme once more on strings and glockenspiel, a bell for each station, a choir on the title, a riser
  as the butterfly flies into the lens, a final D add9 chord with timpani and bells, a four-note celesta coda.
- Layered over all of this, scene by scene: a PIANO (stretched inharmonic partials on two detuned strings, felt
  hammer) greets the dawn, warms the egg, doubles the nightfall lullaby, pulses under the strings as the wings
  pump up, ticks like clockwork through the generations, plays a few hushed notes in the roost and accompanies
  the journey home; TAIKO drums and dark FRENCH HORNS for danger (the jay), for the first flight, driving the
  migration south and for the burst and the final chord.

SOUND DESIGN
- Real recordings (CC0, Joseph Sardin, BigSoundBank): dawn birdsong, countryside, forest birds, wind in tall grass,
  wind in the trees (Mexico), a night meadow with crickets, two Eurasian jay calls for the bird.
- Synthesized: wing flutters, a roar of thousands of wings, leaf munching, shell and chrysalis cracks, skin rustles,
  rubbery wriggles, a zipper, latex drips, thuds and boings, silk shimmer, heartbeat, scanner sweeps, clock ticks,
  pops, sparkles, whooshes, risers, reverse cymbals and impacts; a bumblebee buzz panned with the bee; distant
  goose honks moving across the stereo field with the V; two deep wing beats and a rush of air for each giant-wing
  wipe, a roar of wings for the swarm wipes and a wind gust with a soft choir for the cloud wipe.

HOW TO REBUILD

- Contact sheet of moments:   node tools/render.js --times 2,20,45 --out /tmp/sheet.png

====================================================================================================================
PART 2 — HOW IT WAS PROMPTED
====================================================================================================================

1. "Do you know what is Monarch butterfly?"
   -> A short explanation: Danaus plexippus; milkweed specialist (toxic, warning colours); egg -> caterpillar ->
      chrysalis -> adult; the multigenerational migration to Mexico and California; male scent spots;
      conservation.

2. "Do you know the life cycle of monarch butterfly? Make a video, maybe two minutes, covering the whole end-to-end
   life cycle with every important detail. It should be a motion animated video with very good animation and
   storytelling, a very good background, and background music that changes with the situation. Take reference from
   the chicken video and the amoeba motion video, and make the animation, motion and music better than those two —
   colourful, with music based on the situation. Go all in."
   -> This film (v1).

3. "The animation is good but I want it to be better in detail, with very very good animation; also the trees are
   not looking good. Go all in and make it very nice: very good animation, motion design and music, based on the
   situation."
   -> v2: painted tree sprites everywhere (fuller lit crowns, woodland edges, groves, hedgerows, autumn colours,
      oyamel firs, the roost close-up), a perspective aerial autumn countryside, layered atmospheric haze, sprite
      clouds tinted by the hour, lens flares, motion-design wipes, a bee, birds and geese, dew, crumbs, living
      antennae and flight bob; the score gained piano, horns and taiko, a bee buzz, goose honks and wipe sounds.
